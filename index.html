    Two inks, nothing else

    Every line is one of two colours, so a screen printer burns two screens per placement instead of matching a gradient. PDFs carry real CMYK values and a Pantone reference, not a screen-RGB guess.

    The QR square is a knockout

    Its dark modules are unprinted — the garment shows through them. Fill them in and the code stops scanning, so the spec sheet says so in the largest words on the page.

    Type is outlined

    Every character ships as a filled contour, so there is no font to install and nothing to substitute. The most common way artwork arrives wrong is already ruled out.

    This was one shirt

    I designed it to wear to interviews: my title across the shoulders, the query underneath, a QR square linking to my work. Then people started asking for their own, and a design that only works for one person is a worse design than one that works for everyone.

    So I measured the original artwork down to a hundredth of a millimetre, worked out the rule behind every type size on it, and rebuilt it as this. One rule turned out to govern the whole thing: every automatically sized line fills 99% of its column. That 1% is why the last letter never touches the trim edge.

    The test suite regenerates my shirt from those rules and compares it against the original file, edge by edge. It passes to within 0.01 mm.

    — Yazan Ali, who is still available.
